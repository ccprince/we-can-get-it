# ADR 0001: Galaxy change is a budget, not per-want rates

**Status:** accepted, 2026-10-13
**Context:** `.scratch/changing-galaxy/spec.md` (the spec this decision settles), story 39 of `.scratch/smallest-playable/spec.md`

_The `.scratch/` specs and tickets cited above went with the `schlepper` prototype and no longer exist; the decision stands on its own._


## Context

Changing needs answered story 39 — "I want wants to change over a run, so that the galaxy doesn't feel like a fixed puzzle" — with two per-entity chances in the content settings: `newWantChance`, rolled once per buyer per jump, and `wantExpiryChance`, rolled once per open want per jump.

It worked, and it was far too loud. Three play-tests reported the same thing in the same words: every landing brought three or four expiry and recreation messages (seed 300082 on 2026-10-10, the run on 2026-10-11, seed 950669 on 2026-10-12).

Three attempts to shift it failed, and all three addressed **delivery** rather than generation:

- **19**, a rethink of how contacts enter the game;
- **20**, holding a buyer's wants until the player visited — whose premise, that an overwhelmed player would stop visiting, play-testing proved false: the player visited every buyer as fast as possible regardless;
- **21** and **23**, hidden buyers and leads — which delayed the flood without shrinking it. (They succeeded on their own terms: leads and introductions are the best-working mechanic in the game.)

A fourth attempt, tuning the two rates, was on the open list from 2026-10-11 and is what this ADR rejects.

## Decision

Galaxy change is generated by a **galaxy-wide budget per jump**, not by per-entity chances. `newWantChance` and `wantExpiryChance` are removed.

```
changesPerJump: 2
changeWeights: { newWant: 2, buyerQuiet: 1, stockArrives: 1.5, stockPickedOver: 1.5 }
watchLimit: 5
```

Four event kinds, drawn against those weights:

- **a buyer takes on a new want** — weighted 3:1 toward buyers the player knows, so a new want is a sub-goal they can act on;
- **a buyer goes quiet** — the buyer, not the want, leaves the market. Want identity is preserved: the same wants come back, minus anything already satisfied. The spell's end is scheduled when it starts and costs no budget, the way wind-down already schedules expiry. Buyers with steady wants (the dump buyers) are exempt;
- **a lot arrives** at a shop, from a reserve of pool lots held back by a `lots` cap;
- **a lot is picked over** — quantity drops without the player, down to selling out.

Only **counted-on** changes are announced. A want or buyer is counted on when the player knows of it and at least one of: they carry matching cargo; it was named in a lead; they have sold to that want this run; they are watching it. **Watch** is the explicit form of the same declaration, capped at `watchLimit`, and applies to lots as well as buyers. There is no mute.

| Event | Announced when |
|---|---|
| New want | matching cargo, or watched |
| Buyer goes quiet (wind-down) | counted-on, or watched |
| Buyer returns | watched only |
| Lot arrives | never |
| Lot picked over / sold out | watched only |

Everything else is found by landing, by visiting, or in the trade guide, which stays live and galaxy-wide. The winding-down warning is kept exactly as built; it fires for a smaller set. Announcements become structured values returned from `act`, computed in the engine, not diffed in the view.

## Why not tune the rates

Volume under the old mechanism is a product of the content's size:

```
news per jump  ≈  buyers × wants per buyer × chance per jump
```

With this content set — 5 locations, 21 buyers, `newWantChance: 30`, `wantExpiryChance: 5` — that is roughly 6 new wants and 2 wind-down starts per jump, about 8 events, every one of them demand-side. The prototype is already too loud at five locations, and the full game is meant to be larger. Any pair of rates tuned against this content is wrong for the next content set, and the per-want chance that keeps a large galaxy quiet is low enough that an individual want effectively never changes, which deletes the feature by another route.

So the requirement is a volume that is a **tuned constant, independent of how much content exists**. That is not reachable from per-entity chances by choosing better numbers. Measuring the old rates (ticket 24) is still worth doing, re-pointed as a before/after harness that counts events generated, events announced, and announcements per landing — but its numbers must not be used to tune the two chances into an acceptable range, because succeeding at that on this content set would hide the scaling problem rather than fix it.

## Why announcement is filtered rather than reduced

Three jobs were conflated in one mechanism. Making a known plan risky (the race) is proven in play and needs a small fraction of the events. Keeping the galaxy from being a solved puzzle between runs is already done by the per-run shuffle, and mid-run churn adds nothing to it. Generating new demand-side sub-goals during a run is the only job that needs recreation, and it was firing everywhere at once with no regard for where the player was or what they carried.

The tone problem is the same mistake seen from the player's side: nearly every notice was bad news about something the player was not doing. The game reported state changes; it should report changes to options the player had reason to count on. Nothing is hidden by the filter — every want the player knows of stays in their contacts as last known and is corrected on arrival, as `content/README.md` already promises. Only the announcement goes. This is not a revert to ticket 20, which withheld the wants themselves.

## Why supply-side change is included

Within a run, supply never changed: stock only decreased, and only by the player's own hand. Every bit of "the galaxy is alive" was carried by demand, which is why demand-side churn was pushed until it became noise. In this prototype commerce is the only possible driver of aliveness — no quests, no random events, and travel is jump-from-anywhere-to-anywhere — so both sides have to share the load. Supply-side change is also discovered rather than announced, since the trade guide is already the planning surface, and it feeds the sub-goal shapes demand-side churn ignores (of the seventeen in `.scratch/the-hunt/shapes.md`, only four are driven by what buyers want).

One correction to the first draft of that argument: a lot selling out to someone else **is** a plan broken behind the player's back, exactly like an expired want, so it is filtered like one rather than exempted as "not a loss."

## Consequences

- The two settings disappear from `content/galaxy.json` and from the content checks; the shop pools gain one lot each, with `lots` capped at today's pool size so run-opening stock is unchanged and the whole addition is reserve.
- Prices do not move mid-run. The shop price stays the one forward-looking number readable from away, which matters while "every sale is still done on spec" is an open finding.
- Shops opening and closing is **not** built: the prototype has no authored-event mechanism and that is a larger build than this change warrants. It stays design intent for the full game, along with a second consignment of an already-stocked good (which the duplicate-lot rule in the content checks currently forbids).
- An adaptive split, where the galaxy favours whichever side the player has been ignoring, is rejected for now: it is rubber-banding, and it would make a mis-set budget indistinguishable from the adaptation.
- Ticket 25 (un-gate the wants) is closed into this work rather than played separately, since the gate off with the old generation is the loudest possible configuration.
- The announcement rule becomes engine code under `npm test`, where the old news-diffing in `src/ui/main.ts` was untestable.
- The risk this change runs is the opposite of the one it fixes: a galaxy that reads as calm may read as dead. The play-test brackets it — two runs on one seed, `changesPerJump` at 2 and at 4. **Played 2026-10-21 (changing-galaxy 08): the risk was real at 2, and the content set now ships `changesPerJump: 4`.** The `2` in the Decision above is the value the budget was introduced with, not the tuned one; the mechanism is what this ADR decides, and the number is content.
