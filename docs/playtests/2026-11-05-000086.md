### Bracket seeds: all 200

**Every seed draws the same galaxy at all three settings**: same start, locations, lanes, kinds, goods and
people, compared through `drawGalaxy`. The day files agree too, on location, transit, credits, places known
and lots seen, on every day of every seed. So the shops match. Strictly, though, **no `.draw.csv` row is identical** between the base and either variant. The
shape columns (`motifs`, `locations`, `kinds`, `goods`, `usableKinds`, `goodsPerUsableKind`) are identical
on all 200 seeds. The vague-answer buckets differ on every seed, because they count the wants that
changed. The galaxy comparison above is the test that decides a bracket.

Why: all 8 kinds are usable in every draw, so no vague want at a dropped kind forces a reroll. No
conversion adds or removes a named good. The draw never sees the change, so **any seed is a bracket seed**
for any pair of these settings.

### Vague-answer buckets, per drawn galaxy

`vagueZero`, `vagueOne`, `vagueFew` (2–4) and `vagueMany` (5+): vague wants held by drawn people, bucketed by how many drawn goods
answer them at acceptable or better.

| Setting | zero | one | few | many | seeds with a one-answer vague want |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `plain-and-vague` | 0/0/0 | 0/0/0 | 2/4/7 | 5/7/11 | 1 |
| `plain-leaning` | 0/0/0 | 0/0/0 | 0/2/3 | 2/4/6 | 0 |
| `vague-leaning` | 0/0/0 | 0/0/0 | 2/4/7 | 9/12/16 | 1 |

No setting produces the prototype's lookup: no zero-answer vague want appears, and a one-answer want
turns up in one galaxy of 200. vague-leaning's extra wants land almost all in **many**. Eleven of them
belong to minor buyers with no bonus or refusal tags, so every drawn good of the kind answers them.
Those eleven count as "many" by construction, and they are not the same thing as a tagged vague want
with five answers.

**Other counts:**
- `ordersOpen` and `ordersFillable` are identical across the settings, on every route. The 22 orders
  didn't move, and the draw is the same, so this is the floor holding still as intended.
- `ordersOpenVague` is 0 on every row: every order is plain.
- `goodsUnwanted` moves by about one good between the variants (`explore`, ×20, day 20 median: 5 base, 6
  plain-leaning, 4 vague-leaning). Thin demand barely depends on this division.
- `lotsMatching / lotsSeen` rises through a campaign as known wants accumulate. vague-leaning sits
  2–6 points above the base, and plain-leaning 3–7 below. A vague want matches any lot of its kind.

**Deferred, and why:**

**Closed** _(so it is not picked back up by accident)_:

**Was there enough of this to judge it?** Asked of each variant's shape, as #148 requires:

- **plain-leaning thins vague wants below the 2–4 a shape needs.** In the drawn galaxy, **87% of (seed,
  kind) pairs hold fewer than 2 vague wants**: 639 hold none, 758 hold one, and 203 hold two, of 1,600. Those counts are of the drawn galaxy. Of what the
  tourist is actually shown, the per-kind split is not in the CSV, only the total: the median by day 20 is **3 vague wants in all**, across 8 usable kinds, with
  `explore` at ×20 credits. On 9 of 200 seeds a real-credits explorer sees **no** vague want. So
  plain-leaning cannot show whether a vague want of a given kind works. It can only show what a campaign
  with very few of them feels like, which is the question it was built to ask.
- **The base is already thin per kind.** 53% of (seed, kind) pairs hold fewer than 2 vague wants (246 none,
  598 one), and a ×20 explorer sees a median of 5 vague wants across 8 kinds by day 20. A verdict on "vague
  wants of kind X" is starved at every setting. Read verdicts about vague wants **in general**, not by kind.
- **vague-leaning** brings that down to 34% (135 none, 405 one), with a median of 7 vague wants seen by day
  20 at ×20 credits. Even so, per kind it is still under 1 seen.
